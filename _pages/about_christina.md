Christina Haag is a Research Associate in the Human-Centered Health AI group. Her research brings together psychology and computer science, using computational methods such as AI to deepen the understanding of mental health and to improve it.

Christina studied psychology at the University of Bielefeld and obtained her PhD in clinical psychology from the University of Zurich in 2020. During her doctorate she spent a year and a half at the MRC Cognition and Brain Sciences Unit, University of Cambridge, on a research project funded by a Swiss National Science Foundation mobility fellowship. 

She then returned to the University of Zurich (2020–2024, Institute for Implementation Science in Health Care) to research at the intersection of computer science and epidemiology, with the aim of making patients' own accounts – free text, narratives and clinical records – usable as data for health research. To this end, she developed NLP and language model pipelines on the university's science cluster, and co-led ALPACA (AI-enabled Language Processing of Adolescents' and Young Adults' Challenges and Aspirations). This project used language models to analyse open-text accounts from young people in the Z-proso cohort, including their hopes and worries about the future.

Since early 2024 she has been a researcher in the Digital Mental Health group at the MRC Cognition and Brain Sciences Unit, University of Cambridge, where her research investigates the mechanisms through which digital media affect adolescents' mental health in daily life. Combining data donation – adolescents' own social media watch histories donated to researchers – with experience sampling, she uses multimodal AI-based methods such as computer vision and large language models to measure what young people actually see on social media and to link it to their mental wellbeing.

Since joining Idiap in November 2026, Christina is part of the GALENOS project (Global Alliance for Living Evidence on aNxiety, depressiOn and pSychosis), a Wellcome-funded international initiative that keeps the evidence on these conditions continuously up to date through living systematic reviews; she contributes to the AI-based methods that support this evidence synthesis.

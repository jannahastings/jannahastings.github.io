Dr. Anna Zanina is affiliated with the Human-Centered Health AI research group as an external researcher and specializes in computer-generated language and human-computer interaction.

She holds a PhD in Organizational Studies and Cultural Theory from the University of St. Gallen, Switzerland, and has completed postdoctoral research at the University of North Texas in Denton, United States. She collaborates with international research networks such as "Language in the Human-Machine Era" - an EU interdisciplinary "Cooperation in Science and Technology Network" contributing to advancing knowledge of language's role in human-machine interfaces. Since 2024, she has been actively participating as a speaker and session chair at the International Conference on Human-Computer Interaction (HCI).

Currently, Anna is a lecturer at the University of St. Gallen's Centre for Languages and Transcultural Communication and advises research projects related to language and AI. Her research bridges the gap between language theory and technological innovation to promote comprehensive communication.

One particular focus is on identifying and mitigating the risks posed by persuasive or misleading language, as well as by algorithmically driven conversational patterns.
